# Decalogue reference

Sources: Exodus 20:1–17 and Deuteronomy 5:6–21.

## Numbering

This skill uses the **Philonic numbering** (Reformed, Anglican, Eastern Orthodox; the Jewish numbering is close). Catholic and Lutheran numbering differs: it merges 1–2 and splits 10, so false witness is the 8th there. When the user signals a different tradition, use theirs and say which numbering you're using.

## The commandments and their scope

For each commandment, "Goal/means conflicts" lists typical request patterns that genuinely conflict. "Not a conflict" lists things that are commonly over-flagged. Stay within standard interpretation and don't stretch.

### 1. No other gods before me (Exod 20:2–3; Deut 5:6–7)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** content whose purpose is to lead someone to worship another deity or to put something else (money, a person, the state) in God's place *as an object of worship*. Rituals invoking other gods, performed as real worship.
- **Not a conflict:** studying, describing or comparing religions; fiction or mythology; history of religion; ordinary ambition.
- **Paths:** academic/descriptive framing; the user's own tradition's resources.

### 2. No graven images / idols for worship (Exod 20:4–6; Deut 5:8–10)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** creating objects or imagery *intended for worship*, or designing devotional practice directed at an image.
- **Not a conflict:** art, illustration, sculpture, logos, photography, religious art in traditions that permit it (note: traditions differ on images; don't flag ordinary art).
- **Paths:** art for aesthetic, educational or commemorative purposes.

### 3. Do not take the Lord's name in vain (Exod 20:7; Deut 5:11)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** false oaths sworn in God's name; using God's name or religious authority to lend false credibility (e.g., "God told me this investment is guaranteed" as a sales tactic); mocking God's name as the content's aim.
- **Not a conflict:** quoting, discussing or analyzing blasphemy; theology; characters' speech in fiction (you may note it if prominent, not flag it).
- **Paths:** make the claim on its own merits; remove the invoked religious authority.

### 4. Remember the Sabbath day (Exod 20:8–11; Deut 5:12–15)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** schemes whose design is to force others (employees, servants) to work without any rest day; planning that eliminates rest for oneself or others as a principle.
- **Not a conflict:** a single task done on a weekend; works of necessity and mercy (medicine, emergency services, caring for people and animals); traditions differ on Saturday/Sunday and on application. Mention lightly at most; don't flag routine scheduling.
- **Paths:** build in a weekly rest day; rotation schedules.

### 5. Honor your father and mother (Exod 20:12; Deut 5:16)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** content designed to demean, defraud, abandon or exploit one's parents (e.g., manipulating an elderly parent out of assets); public humiliation of parents as the goal.
- **Not a conflict:** setting boundaries with parents; reporting abuse; honest disagreement; estate planning done openly.
- **Paths:** honest conversation scripts; mediation; transparent legal arrangements.

### 6. You shall not murder (Exod 20:13; Deut 5:17)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** planning to kill or seriously injure a person; incitement to violence; the standard interpretation extends to hatred aimed at harm.
- **Not a conflict:** self-defense training; fiction; history; journalism about violence; lawful military or police topics discussed descriptively; hunting.
- **Paths:** de-escalation, legal protection (restraining orders), authorities.
- (Most such requests also hit hard model limits. Report both separately.)

### 7. You shall not commit adultery (Exod 20:14; Deut 5:18)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** helping arrange, conceal or pursue an affair by a married person or with a married person; content aimed at breaking up a marriage through seduction.
- **Not a conflict:** relationship advice, divorce procedure, romance fiction, sex education.
- **Paths:** honest conversation with the spouse; counseling; lawful separation before new relationships.

### 8. You shall not steal (Exod 20:15; Deut 5:19)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** theft, fraud, embezzlement, piracy of paid content, wage theft, plagiarism presented as own work, scams, deceptive pricing designed to take money without fair exchange, evading debts owed.
- **Not a conflict:** competitive business; lawful tax minimization; using open-licensed or public-domain material; security research with authorization.
- **Paths:** licensing, attribution, fair contracts, legal routes to recover what the user is owed.

### 9. You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor (Exod 20:16; Deut 5:20)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** lies that harm or mislead others, including defamation; fabricated evidence, reviews, testimonials or credentials; deceptive marketing claims; impersonation; misleading omissions designed to deceive; fake confessions; lying in legal or official proceedings.
- **Not a conflict:** fiction clearly presented as fiction; persuasive writing that is truthful; negotiation that withholds one's own bottom line without asserting falsehoods; satire recognizable as satire.
- **Paths:** state only what evidence supports; collect real testimonials; disclose limitations; argue from true facts.

### 10. You shall not covet (Exod 20:17; Deut 5:21)
- **Goal/means conflicts:** this commandment addresses desire. Flag only when the *request itself* is aimed at acquiring what belongs to a neighbor (their spouse, house, business, employee) by illegitimate means, or when the content is designed to stoke envy of a specific neighbor's possessions as a manipulation tactic.
- **Not a conflict:** ambition, wanting to buy a house, competing fairly, aspirational marketing in general. Don't police the user's inner desires.
- **Paths:** fair offers, open competition, earning rather than taking.

## How to assess a prompt

1. **Goal**: what outcome does the user want? Is that outcome itself forbidden by a commandment? Most goals (earn money, win the case, finish the code) are fine.
2. **Means**: how would the requested output achieve it? This is where most conflicts sit (e.g., a fine goal of "sell more" pursued by false claims → 9th).
3. **Specificity test**: can you name the commandment, the verse, and the exact element of the request that conflicts in one line each? If not, it's not a conflict. Report `no conflict`.
4. **Alternatives**: for each conflict, offer 2–3 concrete paths that reach the same goal without the conflicting means. Tools, templates, procedures, not exhortation.

## Tone

Factual and brief. Cite, don't preach. The user decides; you report.
